StyloSynth555

Touch a brass stylus to twenty copper keys. A 555 timer sings, a binary counter keeps the octaves perfect, and an LM386 drives the speaker. The schematic, PCB, enclosure, simulations and this web app all come from one Python circuit description.

Backstory

Sixty years of drawing music with a pen

1967

The Stylophone

Brian Jarvis's pocket instrument for Dubreq: a metal stylus on printed-circuit keys, one buzzy oscillator and a vibrato switch. Cheap, odd and unforgettable. It made it onto David Bowie's “Space Oddity” in 1969.

since

The 555 clones

Makers have rebuilt the Stylophone around the humble 555 timer ever since: a handful of resistors, a stylus and a speaker. Those DIY clones, and the problems their builders wrote up, are what inspired this project.

2026

StyloSynth-555

The idea taken further around a CMOS 555: a regulated oscillator rail, exact octaves from a binary counter, a proper power amp and two-trimmer calibration. Every artefact is generated by scripts and checked automatically before a board is ever ordered.

How it works

From a touch to a tone

Stylus + 20 keysa resistor ladder: each key is a different timing resistance
TLC555square-wave oscillator, 880 Hz to 2.6 kHz
CD4040divides by 2, 4, 8 or 16: four exact octaves
Tone + volumean RC low-pass softens the square wave
LM386gain 20 into an 8 Ω speaker or headphones
LM358 LFO3.9–9.6 Hz wobble into the 555's CV pin: vibrato
9 V → LP2950 5 Va quiet rail for the oscillator, raw 9 V for the amp

Built from code

One source of truth, eight generated outputs

  1. circuit.pyevery part and net, the single source of truth
  2. Ladder mathsE96 resistors chosen so rounding never accumulates, plus a 2000-build tolerance analysis
  3. ngspiceoscillator, LFO and audio path simulated against the design maths
  4. SchematicKiCad sheet generated, netlist round-trip verified
  5. PCBplaced by script, routed with Freerouting, DRC clean
  6. BOM + fabGerbers, drill, pick-and-place, STEP and a priced BOM
  7. Enclosuresmanifold STLs and laser-cut DXFs, checked against the board for fit
  8. This web appa whole-board SPICE bench and a real-time model calibrated against it

Demo mode: the simulation server isn't connected.

Bench runs a full ngspice simulation of the circuit for every change, which needs the Python server. What you see here is one recorded run: key 13 (A5), vibrato on, a 1 s capture. Every probe point, check and scope trace, plus the speaker audio, is real, but nothing can be re-simulated.

To simulate live, run .venv/bin/python bench/server.py from the project folder and open http://127.0.0.1:8555/#bench. This page reconnects automatically when the server appears.
